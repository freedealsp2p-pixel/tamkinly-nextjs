Cognitive Distortions Reference
• All-or-Nothing Thinking: Seeing situations in extremes with little room for middle ground.
• Emotional Reasoning: Treating an emotion as proof that a belief or interpretation is fact.
• Overgeneralization: Drawing a broad conclusion from a limited event or pattern.
• Should Statements: Using rigid expectations about what you or others "should" do.
• Mind Reading: Assuming you know what another person is thinking without sufficient evidence.
• Discounting the Positive: Minimizing information that does not fit the negative interpretation.
• Catastrophizing: Focusing on the most threatening possible outcome as if it is certain.
• Fortune Telling: Predicting a negative future outcome without enough evidence.
• Personalization: Taking excessive responsibility for something not fully in your control.

Developing a Balanced Thought

Use what you noticed on the previous page to create a perspective that is realistic, evidence-informed, and compassionate.

1. Balanced Thought
Guidance: A balanced thought does not have to be positive. It should acknowledge difficult information while also making room for other evidence, uncertainty, context, and personal agency. It should not deny real danger, abuse, or boundary violations.
2. Belief Rating — After Reframing

3. Compassionate Observer Perspective

Compassion does not mean denying what happened. It means responding without unnecessary self-attack.

Educational Disclaimer

This worksheet is for educational and supportive purposes. It does not provide a diagnosis and does not replace individualized psychological assessment, psychotherapy, medical care, or other professional support. Thought reframing is not intended to deny, minimize, or reinterpret away real danger, abuse, or boundary violations. If a situation is currently unsafe, prioritize safety and appropriate professional or emergency support.
