## Audience

## Required Sections
### Page 1
- Title header with asset name
- Purpose statement
- FIGHT response:
  - Body signals (clenched jaw, fists, heat, agitation)
  - Emotional signature (anger, rage, defensiveness)
  - Behavioral pattern (confrontation, control, criticism)
  - Deactivation strategies (grounding, movement discharge, breath)
- FLIGHT response:
  - Body signals (restless legs, urge to leave, rapid heart)
  - Emotional signature (anxiety, fear, urgency)
  - Behavioral pattern (avoidance, over-busy, perfectionism)
  - Deactivation strategies (slow down, present-moment anchoring)
- FREEZE response:
  - Body signals (numbness, stillness, shallow breath, dissociation)
  - Emotional signature (shut down, blank, detached)
  - Behavioral pattern (isolation, inability to act, compliance)
  - Deactivation strategies (gentle movement, sensory stimulation, orientation)
- FAWN response:
  - Body signals (tension in pleasing, ignoring own needs)
  - Emotional signature (guilt, shame, fear of rejection)
  - Behavioral pattern (people-pleasing, over-accommodation, loss of boundaries)
  - Deactivation strategies (boundary practice, self-advocacy, centering)

### Page 2
- Quick identification: Which response is dominant right now? (checklist)
- Mixed responses note (multiple responses can co-occur)
- Decision guide: If fight → use X, If flight → use Y, If freeze → use Z, If fawn → use W
- Escalation pathway: when self-regulation is not enough
- Emergency contacts template
- Footer with tool ID and route

## Safety Requirements
- When to use: Understanding own response patterns, choosing appropriate regulation tool, psychoeducation
- When to stop: Identifying patterns triggers shame or self-blame, information becomes overwhelming
- When not appropriate: Acute crisis, using to diagnose self or others, substituting for therapeutic guidance
- When additional support needed: Patterns feel unchangeable/hopeless, recognizing responses without ability to regulate
- Escalation language: "If recognizing your patterns feels overwhelming, remember: your responses kept you safe. They are not flaws. Reach out for support."
