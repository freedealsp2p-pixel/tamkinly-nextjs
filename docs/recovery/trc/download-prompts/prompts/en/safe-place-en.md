## 6. 📄 Page Content

### Page 1
- **Title:** "Safe Place Visualization"
- **Purpose:** "This worksheet helps you create a mental safe place — a location, real or imagined, where you feel calm, secure, and at peace. You can return to this place in your mind whenever you need to feel grounded and safe."
- **Safety Alert Box** (bg: #FFFBEB, border: #D97706, text: #92400E): "Safety Notice: If visualization causes distress, flashbacks, or increased anxiety, stop immediately. Open your eyes, look around your physical space, and name 5 things you can see. This tool is not a substitute for professional care."
- **Instructions — 5 Steps:**
  1. "Find a comfortable position. Close your eyes if it feels safe, or keep them softly focused."
  2. "Think of a place where you feel completely safe and calm. It can be real (a place you have been) or imagined (a place you create)."
  3. "Notice the details of this place using all five senses — what do you see, hear, smell, touch, and taste?"
  4. "Let yourself be fully present in this safe place for a few moments. Notice how your body feels when you are there."
  5. "When you are ready, gently open your eyes (if closed) and write about your safe place on the next page."

## 7. 🛑 Safety Section
- **When to use:** When needing a mental refuge from stress, anxiety, or emotional pain. When a calm internal resource would help with emotional regulation.
- **When to stop:** If visualization triggers flashbacks, if distress increases, if no safe place can be identified, if dissociation occurs after the exercise.
- **Not appropriate for:** Individuals with active PTSD where visualization may trigger traumatic memories; individuals with psychosis where imagined scenarios may blur with reality; individuals who find eye-closing unsafe.
- **Escalation:** If visualization consistently triggers distress, discontinue and use sensory grounding instead. Consult a trauma-informed therapist for guided safe place work.
