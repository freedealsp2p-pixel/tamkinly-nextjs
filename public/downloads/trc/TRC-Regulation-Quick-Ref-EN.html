Regulation Quick Reference

Present-focused grounding and nervous system stabilization options for moments of acute distress.
This card is for stabilization, not exposure or trauma processing. Choose what feels manageable. You do not need to make the distress disappear, identify its cause, or complete an exercise.
Safety Notice
When distress is intense, keep the task simple. Choose ONE present-focused option and stay with it only while it feels tolerable. Keep your attention connected to your surroundings. Stop if an exercise increases distress, dizziness, panic, dissociation, pain, or disorientation.
You are not required to push through discomfort. A simpler grounding or supportive contact is a valid next step.
Quick Tool Selector — choose the option that best matches what you notice
High Anxiety / Activation
A52 Breathing & Mild Cooling
Use only if breathing-based regulation feels comfortable and familiar. Keep breathing natural and unforced. If using cooling, choose a mild and physically safe sensation rather than extreme cold.
Stop: If breathing becomes uncomfortable, dizziness appears, distress increases, or you feel less oriented.
Dissociation / Feeling Disconnected
5-4-3-2-1 Grounding & Gentle Movement
Start with external orientation: notice concrete details you can see, hear, and physically feel. Gentle movement may be added if it helps you reconnect with the present environment.
Stop: If focusing inward, movement, or sensory attention increases disconnection or distress.
Emotional Overwhelm
Safe Place / Supportive Imagery
If imagery feels comfortable, use a familiar, neutral, or supportive setting. Keep attention on the present. You may skip imagery entirely if it feels unfamiliar or uncomfortable.
Stop: If imagery becomes intrusive, emotionally activating, or pulls you away from the present.
Somatic Tension
Body Awareness & Gentle Movement
Notice areas of tension without forcing relaxation. If physically comfortable, try a small posture change, gentle stretch, walking, or other familiar movement.
Stop: If body-focused attention increases distress, pain, dissociation, dizziness, or disorientation.
These are optional self-regulation supports. They are not diagnostic tests, trauma-processing techniques, or substitutes for professional care.

Stabilization Protocols

A simple sequence for returning attention to the present and deciding when additional support is needed.
Use the sequence flexibly. The purpose is to increase present-moment orientation and safety, not to force symptoms to stop. If self-regulation is not helping, moving toward support is part of stabilization.
01 — Pause & Orient
  • Pause: If it is safe to do so, stop or slow the activity you are doing.
  • Look outward: Place attention on the room or environment around you.
  • Notice a few concrete details you can see, hear, or physically feel.
  • Remind yourself: "I am here, in the present moment."
  • You do not need to explain, analyze, or revisit what happened.
02 — Choose One
  • Choose one option from Page 1 that feels manageable.
  • Keep the approach gentle and present-focused.
  • Do not force breathing, sensations, emotions, memories, or relaxation.
  • You may stop at any point.
  • If the strategy makes things worse, stop rather than pushing through.
03 — Reconnect & Support
  • Reconnect: Move toward a familiar, safer environment when possible.
  • Consider contacting a trusted support person.
  • If you already have a professional stabilization plan, follow that plan.
  • If self-regulation is not enough, seek appropriate support.
When Self-Regulation Is Not Enough
Self-regulation tools are not crisis care. Move toward professional, crisis, or emergency support when the situation is severe, unsafe, or cannot be managed adequately with available support.
Immediate danger or inability to maintain personal safety.
Severe or rapidly increasing disorientation or dissociation.
Inability to regain basic orientation to the present environment.
Severe panic or distress that remains difficult to manage.
Immediate risk of harm to yourself or another person.
A situation requiring urgent medical or mental-health assessment.
If you are in immediate danger or facing a mental-health emergency, do not rely on this card. Prioritize immediate safety and contact appropriate local emergency or crisis support.
