Support & Safety Guide

Guidance for increased distress and when to pause or stop

TRC
Support Guide
Companion Back
💡 When you need additional grounding
  • If this exercise does not help right away, that does not mean you are doing anything wrong. Returning attention to the present moment can take time, and another form of support may be more helpful.
  • You can try placing your feet firmly on the floor or using another simple sensory action that feels safe and comfortable for you.
  • If disconnection from the present moment or mental distraction continues, try noticing several things around you that share a particular color.
  • If intense distress continues, contact a trusted person or an appropriate support service.
📌 General guidance
  • Directing attention toward your senses and immediate surroundings may help reduce distraction and support a return to the present moment.
  • Do not rush through the exercise. Take the time you need with each sense, without forcing yourself to achieve a particular result.
⛔ When to pause or stop
Guidance for use and stopping
  • Stop the exercise or choose another support tool if your distress is so intense that you cannot comfortably focus on your senses or surroundings.
  • Stop immediately if focusing on your senses or surroundings increases your distress or does not feel appropriate for you at that moment.
When additional support is needed
  • If the discomfort or sense of disconnection does not ease, take a break and move to another support tool.
  • If thoughts of self-harm arise, seek immediate emergency help or contact an appropriate support service.
➡️ Next step
After completing the exercise, you can return to the Tamkinly TRC recovery journey and choose another support tool that fits your current needs through the platform.
