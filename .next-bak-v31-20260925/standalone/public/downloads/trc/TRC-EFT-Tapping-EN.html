EFT-Style Tapping Worksheet

A present-focused self-regulation exercise for noticing difficult feelings without needing to revisit their cause.

This worksheet presents tapping as an optional coping practice. It is not a trauma-processing exercise and does not require memory retrieval, exposure, or detailed discussion of what happened.

Safety Notice

Tapping does not work the same way for everyone. Use only gentle, comfortable touch and remain oriented to your surroundings.

Do not use this exercise to force yourself to remember, describe, reinterpret, or process a traumatic experience.

If your distress increases, you become less oriented to the present, or the exercise feels uncomfortable, stop and return to a grounding or stabilization practice that feels appropriate.

You may stop at any point. You do not need to complete every step.

8-Point Tapping Sequence

Use gentle, comfortable tapping while keeping attention on the present.

Tap lightly with your fingertips. There is no required pressure or duration. If any point feels uncomfortable, skip it. The locations below are provided as a practical tapping guide, not as medical treatment points.

Illustration: EFT tapping points on the body

Illustrative location guide

How to use the sequence: move through the points in order at a comfortable pace.

Keep your attention on what you notice now. There is no requirement to name the origin of the feeling or recall an event.

If looking at or touching a particular area feels uncomfortable, skip that point and continue only if remaining oriented and comfortable.

1. Karate Chop Side of the hand. Tap gently.
2. Top of Head Crown of the head. Use light fingertip tapping.
3. Eyebrow Near the inner edge of the eyebrow.
4. Side of Eye Near the outer edge of the eye socket; avoid pressing the eye.
5. Under Eye On the bone beneath the eye; avoid pressure on the eye itself.
6. Under Nose Between the nose and upper lip.
7. Chin On the area between the lower lip and chin.
8. Collarbone On the soft area just below the collarbone; tap gently.

Optional Second Round

Continue only if you remain oriented and the exercise still feels manageable. You may skip another round completely.

Even though I notice ________________________________, I can ______________________________________________.

Keep the statement present-focused. Do not use this section to search for memories, explain the cause of the feeling, or revisit traumatic details.

Permission to Stop

You may end the exercise here. Continuing is optional. If the exercise is increasing distress or reducing your sense of present orientation, stop and use a grounding or stabilization practice that feels appropriate.

Before closing: notice your surroundings and decide whether you feel sufficiently oriented to continue with your day or whether you need additional support.

Closure & Support

Close the exercise gently and return your attention to the present environment.

Close the Exercise

Stop tapping. Let your breathing remain natural and notice the environment around you.

Notice the surface supporting your body, a few ordinary objects, familiar sounds, or other details that help you orient to where you are now.

You do not need to feel completely calm before ending. The goal is to finish in a way that feels manageable and oriented.

When to Stop

Stop the exercise if you experience rapidly increasing distress, panic that is becoming difficult to manage, intrusive flashbacks, severe dissociation, significant disorientation, or a substantial loss of connection with the present moment.

You do not need to push through. Close the worksheet and return to a grounding or stabilization practice that feels appropriate.

If you remain significantly distressed, cannot regain a sense of orientation, or believe you may be unsafe, seek appropriate professional, crisis, or emergency support according to your situation.
