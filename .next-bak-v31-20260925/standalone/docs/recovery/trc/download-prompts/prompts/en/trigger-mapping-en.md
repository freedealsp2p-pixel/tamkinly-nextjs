## 7. 🛑 Safety Section
- **When to use:** When wanting to understand a specific trigger after the event has passed. When trying to identify patterns in emotional reactions. When preparing to discuss triggers with a therapist.
- **When to stop:** If reviewing the trigger becomes overwhelming, if you become stuck in the memory, if shame or self-blame dominates, if you cannot return to the present moment.
- **Not appropriate for:** Immediately after a traumatic event (wait until emotional intensity has decreased); when still in acute crisis; when the person is not in a safe environment; without therapist guidance for individuals with severe PTSD.
- **Escalation:** If trigger mapping consistently leads to re-traumatization or emotional flooding, discontinue self-directed use and work with a trauma-informed therapist on trigger identification within a therapeutic context.
